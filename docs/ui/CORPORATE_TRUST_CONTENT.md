# Corporate Trust and Content Standards

## Why this matters

For a corporate or B2B website, visual polish alone does not establish trust. Visitors need clear, credible information and reliable pathways to verify what the organization does.

## 1. Clarity before promotion

Corporate copy should answer:

- What is Drixil Group?
- What does it do?
- What are its business areas/capabilities?
- Where does it operate, if published?
- Why is it credible?
- How can a visitor engage?

Do not hide core information behind brand poetry.

## 2. Specific beats generic

Prefer specific language:

> Provides X service for Y type of project.

over:

> Delivering innovative world-class solutions for a better tomorrow.

Do not rewrite approved copy casually, but flag unclear or generic text and improve it when the repository gives enough factual context.

## 3. About/company content

Where verified information exists, make company information easy to find.

Useful content may include:

- company overview;
- history;
- leadership;
- values;
- governance;
- operating locations;
- business units/subsidiaries;
- industries;
- certifications;
- contact details.

Do not invent missing content.

## 4. Business/service pages

A strong business/service page should make clear:

- what the capability is;
- who it is for;
- what work is performed;
- differentiators that can be substantiated;
- relevant projects/evidence;
- how to inquire.

Avoid empty cards containing only a title, icon, and vague sentence when more useful approved information exists.

## 5. Projects/case studies

Where project content exists, structure it for evidence:

- project/client name when publishable;
- sector;
- location;
- scope;
- challenge;
- work delivered;
- result/status;
- imagery;
- related capabilities.

Do not infer confidential details.

## 6. Numbers and metrics

Only display numbers that are supported by project data or approved company content.

Never create counters such as:

- `25+ years`;
- `100+ projects`;
- `20 countries`;
- `98% satisfaction`;

unless those figures are verified.

If a number is time-sensitive, include context or avoid presenting it as permanently current.

## 7. Logos and affiliations

Do not add:

- client logos;
- certification logos;
- partner badges;
- award marks;

without verified assets and permission.

Logo walls should not be decorative filler.

## 8. Contact credibility

Where published, keep company contact data consistent across:

- contact page;
- footer;
- structured data;
- page metadata where relevant.

Use real `mailto:`/`tel:` links appropriately.

## 9. Legal and privacy

Do not fabricate legal text.

If privacy, terms, cookie, or compliance pages exist:

- keep them discoverable;
- ensure footer links work;
- do not hide them behind inaccessible menus.

If they do not exist, flag the gap instead of inventing legal policies.

## 10. Writing style

Preferred tone:

- confident;
- precise;
- restrained;
- knowledgeable;
- direct.

Avoid:

- hype;
- excessive exclamation marks;
- buzzword chains;
- false urgency;
- sales-popups on entry;
- vague “future of…” statements unless they are part of approved brand language.

## 11. Link integrity

Links should communicate destination.

Avoid multiple identical `Learn more` links in a list when accessible names do not distinguish destinations.

Prefer:

- `Learn about [business name]`
- `View [project name]`
- `Explore [capability]`

## 12. Credibility audit

For each major claim, ask:

- Is it specific?
- Is it understandable?
- Is it supported?
- Is proof near enough to the claim?
- Is the claim still current?
- Can a visitor verify or explore it?

If not, reduce the claim or improve the supporting context.
