# Visitor Retention and Conversion Guidelines

These rules are designed for a corporate/group website, where visitors often arrive to validate credibility, understand capabilities, compare alternatives, or find a contact path.

## 1. Retention begins with comprehension

A visitor should not need to interpret abstract copy before understanding the company.

Above the fold, prioritize:

1. recognizable brand;
2. clear value/capability statement;
3. concise supporting explanation;
4. one primary next step;
5. enough visual context to establish quality.

Do not optimize the first viewport for maximum novelty at the cost of clarity.

## 2. Put the strongest information early

Important content should not be hidden behind:

- long intro animations;
- huge decorative video;
- oversized whitespace;
- multiple vague brand statements;
- carousel slides that require waiting.

The first two screenfuls should establish relevance and credibility.

## 3. Progressive trust

A useful corporate-page sequence is often:

**Promise → explanation → proof → detail → next action**

For example:

- what Drixil does;
- how its businesses/capabilities are organized;
- selected evidence or experience;
- deeper details;
- contact/engagement CTA.

Do not make unsupported claims and place proof several pages away if verified proof can be shown nearby.

## 4. Scannability

Visitors scan before they read.

Use:

- descriptive headings;
- short paragraphs;
- useful bullets;
- meaningful labels;
- visual grouping;
- strong contrast between heading and body hierarchy.

Avoid:

- walls of text;
- jargon-heavy copy;
- multiple lines of tiny uppercase text;
- repetitive mission-style prose;
- sections that all begin with vague labels.

## 5. CTA strategy

Each section does not need a CTA.

Every CTA should answer: “What happens if I click this?”

Prefer:

- `Explore our businesses`
- `View selected projects`
- `Contact Drixil Group`
- `Discuss a partnership`
- `Read about the group`

Avoid excessive generic:

- `Learn more`
- `Discover`
- `Explore`
- `Get started`

unless the destination is already obvious from nearby context.

## 6. Reduce decision friction

Do not put 3–5 equal-weight buttons together.

Use:

- one primary;
- one secondary when necessary;
- tertiary inline links for lower-priority exploration.

## 7. Contact friction

If the visitor is ready to contact Drixil:

- do not make them hunt through multiple pages;
- provide clear contact routes;
- make inquiry type understandable;
- avoid overly long first-contact forms;
- preserve submitted content if validation fails;
- confirm success clearly.

Do not add chat widgets or popups unless there is an actual support process behind them.

## 8. No dead ends

At the end of major pages provide a context-appropriate continuation:

- related business/service;
- selected project;
- about/company page;
- contact/inquiry.

A page should not simply end after a decorative section.

## 9. Social proof and proof of capability

Use only real, permissioned, verifiable proof.

Higher-value proof is specific:

- named project/case study;
- concrete scope;
- actual result;
- verified certification;
- real partner/client;
- real location;
- company history.

Avoid fabricating counters or using fake “trusted by” logo walls.

## 10. Animation and retention

Animation should improve perceived quality without slowing comprehension.

Avoid:

- delayed hero text;
- staggered entrances on every paragraph;
- scroll-triggered reveals that hide content until JavaScript runs;
- full-screen loading sequences;
- cinematic transitions between ordinary pages;
- auto-advancing carousels containing important content.

## 11. Mobile retention

On mobile:

- compress excess hero height;
- show the value proposition quickly;
- keep navigation simple;
- keep contact paths reachable;
- avoid side-by-side text that becomes cramped;
- ensure media does not dominate multiple screens before meaningful text appears.

## 12. Analytics-aware iteration

If analytics already exists, preserve it.

Useful future measurements may include:

- navigation usage;
- CTA click-through;
- contact-form completion;
- high-exit pages;
- device breakdown;
- page speed by template;
- scroll depth as a supporting signal.

Do not add tracking or invasive analytics without approval.

The agent should improve the interface based on established UX principles and existing product evidence, not invent business conclusions from imagined analytics.
